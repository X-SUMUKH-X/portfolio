strategy · creative execution

how i actually think about creative strategy

i didn’t “help with content.” i thought like a creative strategist and executed like an operator.

most days started the same way: what’s the story we’re telling today, and where does it live — reel, meme, banner, script, or a DM that doesn’t sound like a DM.

fokus

for fokus, it wasn’t “let’s do a banner.” it was: how do we make a sugar-free drink look fun without shouting “healthy” in people’s faces. that turned into a bright, flavour-first offline banner idea that looked like something you’d actually stop and notice, not a pharma ad.

ideas, trends, brand reality

for social, i sat in the middle of ideas, trends, and brand reality. i pitched things like the “stack fokus challenge,” delivery reels, POV shots tracing a product from warehouse to doorstep, and subtle product placements that feel like scenes, not ads.

i spent a lot of time inside other people’s content too — studying what competitors were doing, what was overdone, and what still had white space. not to copy, but to know exactly what not to make.

meme marketing

for shows like “very parivarik” and “lafangey,” i was breaking down specific moments with share potential — family group admin drama, awkward dinner conversations, city chaos, friendship banter — and positioning them as daily internet moments, not “campaign assets.”

same with performance brands like avvatar nutrition: gym fails, protein shake mishaps, “new PR” ego — all the stuff fitness people actually joke about with their friends. that’s the raw material. my job was to connect it to the brand without killing the joke.

outreach was part of the creative strategy

every email or whatsapp message had a clear arc: a real compliment, proof of work, then a bridge into the kind of content we could build around their world. no generic “we’ll increase engagement” line — the work had to answer why you, why now, why us.

the invisible work

internally, i helped turn vague directions into usable creative: tightening campaign names, sharpening hooks, giving notes on edits, and making sure every piece answered three things: what’s the insight, what’s the emotion, what’s the share line.

i wasn’t just shipping posts. i was finding the moments worth amplifying, turning them into formats the internet actually respects, and backing it with communication that didn’t feel written by a robot.
